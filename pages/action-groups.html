Action Group Bible Studies: “Run To Win!”

The official Bible study modules for Camp Meeting 2026. Use this page during breakout sessions to read memory verses, examine discussion questions, and record personal action points.

Preamble from the National Committee

“Run” means to pace faster than a walk. It is a verb that demands action. In a world panting profusely to catch up with technological advancement, Scripture calls us to a different race — not a race to compete against our brothers and sisters, but a track race that demands the discipline of professional athletes for spiritual and eternal ends.

Camp Group Guidelines: Break into assigned circles of 10–15 campers. Each group should appoint a discussion leader and timekeeper. Give every camper the floor to share.

Bible Study One • Friday Session (10:30 AM – 11:30 AM)

Run Differently: Lay Aside Every Weight and Sin

Study 1

Introduction: The Usain Bolt Principle

Usain Bolt! This name evokes memories of extraordinary athletic prowess. This Jamaican sprinter graced the world stage and kept millions of people glued to their sets watching the 2004, 2008, 2012, and 2016 Olympic Games. How did Usain Bolt dress for all those career-defining races in his profession? Was he ever sighted in a three-piece suit and tie on the tracks? No! Does Usain Bolt lack fine suits? Of course not! Then why didn't he ever wear one for those races? Simple answer: they would have made him ineffective.

Memory Verse (Hebrews 12:1 ESV):
“Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us also lay aside every weight, and sin which clings so closely, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us.”

Core Definitions Segment:

  • Weight: A bulk or mass that hinders movement. Any morally neutral pursuit, habit, relationship, or entertainment that weighs down spiritual momentum and distracts spiritual progress.
  • Sin: Missing the mark. Anything thought, said, or done that displeases God and separates a person from His holiness.

Action Group Discussion Questions:

  1. Share your personal thoughts on Hebrews 12:1 in light of the difference between weights and sins.
  2. Revisiting the illustration about Usain Bolt, how would you classify each of the following:
    • Usain Bolt wearing a three-piece suit for the Olympic 100m final (Weight or Sin? Explain why).
    • Usain Bolt taking unapproved performance-enhancing drugs (Weight or Sin? Explain why).
  3. Use the following passages to shed light on how weights and sins lurk around our daily lives:
    • Sin: Romans 13:11–14; 2 Corinthians 7:1; Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:5–8.
    • Weight: Luke 8:14; Luke 12:15; Luke 18:22–25.
  4. Discuss some of the morally neutral habits that are predominantly shaping 21st-century youth (e.g. screen time, social media trends, casual associations).
  5. Take turns in your circle to read Hebrews 11, highlighting the heroes of faith and what weights and sins they had to confront.
Study Conclusion: Weights are morally neutral situations that may not be sinful in and of themselves, but they make the Christian runner ineffective in his testimony for Christ. Lay them aside! TAC NGYN • God first! Prophetic! Transformational!
Bible Study Two • Saturday Morning (10:30 AM – 11:30 AM)

Run Purposefully: No More Shadowboxing

Study 2

Introduction: Joe Frazier and Secret Preparation

Joe Frazier, the American Olympic gold medalist and undisputed world heavyweight boxing champion (1970–1973), once remarked insightfully: “You can map out a fight plan or a life plan, but when the action starts, it may not go the way you planned, and you're down to your reflexes — that means your preparation. That's where your roadwork shows. If you cheated on that in the dark of the morning, well, you're going to get found out now, under the bright lights.”

Key Bible Verse (1 Corinthians 9:26–27 ESV):
“So I do not run aimlessly; I do not box as one beating the air. But I discipline my body and keep it under control, lest after preaching to others I myself should be disqualified.”

Core Definition Segment:

Shadowboxing: To box with an imaginary opponent, especially as a form of training. In spiritual terms: going through empty religious rituals without striking at the real lusts of the flesh; expending sweat without victory.

Action Group Discussion Questions:

  1. Express in your own words what it means to run aimlessly and box as one beating the air.
  2. How does Joe Frazier's quote regarding early morning roadwork apply to a young Christian's secret devotional life and personal prayer?
  3. The Greek word translated “discipline” means to keep down or bring under strict subjection. How can youth practically keep their bodily appetites in subjection to the Spirit?
  4. Identify major hindrances to keeping our bodies under subjection in the 21st century.
  5. What must be on top for the body to remain underneath? (Examine Galatians 5:17–21).
  6. How do we get the body down and the Spirit up? Discuss using:
    • Be filled with the Spirit (Ephesians 5:18)
    • Walk in the Spirit (Galatians 5:16)
    • Obey the Spirit (Ephesians 4:30; 1 Thessalonians 5:19)
  7. Disqualified! What does it mean? How can a gifted Christian be disqualified, and how do we prevent it? (Read 1 Corinthians 10:1–33).
Study Conclusion: The body, left to its own appetites, results in disqualification. Paul appeals to us in Romans 12:1: “Present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship.”
Bible Study Three • Saturday Second Session

Run With Endurance: Finish Well

Study 3

Introduction: John Stephen Akhwari (Mexico 1968)

The year was 1968, and all eyes were on the Olympic Games in Mexico City. John Stephen Akhwari from Tanzania took part in the Men's Marathon — the grueling 26.2-mile race. Due to the high altitude, John suffered severe cramps, fell heavily, crushed his knees picking up a dislocation, and smashed his shoulders against the pavement. Observers assumed he would pull out. But Akhwari pressed on with bloodied bandages.

Over an hour after the winner had received his medal and virtually everyone had left the stadium, Akhwari limped across the finish line. Asked why he persisted through unbearable pain, he uttered his immortal response: “My country did not send me 5,000 miles to start the race. They sent me 5,000 miles to finish the race.”

Key Bible Verse (Hebrews 12:1c ESV):
“...and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us.”

Core Definition Segment:

Endurance: Steadfast endurance that clings to God in the face of pressure. Its essence is a Spirit-empowered tenacity that remains under trial until God's divine purpose is fulfilled.

Action Group Discussion Questions:

  1. Share your thoughts on Hebrews 12:1c in light of the definition of spiritual endurance.
  2. Why does the Christian race demand relentless endurance? Examine these 3 agencies:
    • The devil against the redeemed (1 Peter 5:8)
    • The world system against the redeemed (1 John 2:15–17)
    • The unredeemed flesh against the redeemed (Romans 7:15–20)
  3. Do we have guaranteed victory against these three forces? Read 1 Peter 5:8–11, Romans 12:2, and Romans 6:13 to explain why believers always triumph.
  4. How important is your mindset and perspective when you are going through an unpleasant or discouraging trial?
  5. How can we cultivate a mindset that endures to the end? (Hebrews 12:2–3; 1 Peter 4:1).
  6. Prayerfully write down areas where you sense the Spirit of God calling you to stand firm and finish strong without compromise.
Study Conclusion: Finish your race, irrespective of the challenges that come your way. Your Heavenly Father did not enlist you to start the race, but to finish it victoriously (Philippians 3:20; 2 Timothy 4:7).

Action Group Facilitator Guidelines

  • Punctuality: Begin promptly at the scheduled time (Friday 10:30 AM & Saturday 10:30 AM).
  • Safe Atmosphere: Ensure everyone feels respected and free to share their thoughts without judgment.
  • Keep on Track: Gently steer the conversation back to the scripture verses if discussions drift into side topics.
  • Prayer Finish: Dedicate the last 5–10 minutes for campers to pray together and minister strength to one another.
